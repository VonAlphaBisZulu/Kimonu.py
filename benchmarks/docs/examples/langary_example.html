Langary example network

Metabolites as circles, reactions as labelled arrows, read from the columns of N; forward/reverse pairs merged into one reversible reaction. Where a reaction has more than one substrate or product, the substrate curves merge into one bold stem that then splits to the products, so each fork reads left to right as substrates, reaction, products. Arrowheads point at the species a reaction can produce, so a reversible reaction has arrowheads on both sides. All stoichiometric coefficients are 1.

One crossing is unavoidable: the species–reaction graph is not planar (checked; removing any one of ten edges would make it so). Here it is the E arm of R6/R7 crossing the G arm of R9/R10. E and G (orange) are the shared species: E is consumed in R6/R7 and returned by R11 and R14; G is released by R8 and taken up by R9/R10 and R12/R13.
