Nullspace walkthrough

The example network of Langary et al. (2025) after merging each forward/reverse pair and keeping only feasible directions: R1/R2 carries no net flux in any steady state and is removed, R3, R6, R9 and R12 only run forward. Eight reactions, twelve complexes, nine metabolites. Each step below is one matrix; together they decide balanced complexes and concordance without a single linear program.

1 · The network and its incidence matrix A

A records which complex each reaction consumes (−1) and produces (+1). It is the graph on the left, written as a matrix: one column per arrow. A alone cannot decide balance; that needs what the complexes are made of.

3 · Steady states: K = ker(N)

N has rank 6, so its kernel has 8 − 6 = 2 dimensions. Every steady-state flux is v = K·b. The two columns are the network's two elementary modes: the loop B → C → D (+E) → F → B (+G), returning G to E either through H (k1) or through I (k2).

ker(A) is empty here: the complex graph has no cycle of its own, so both steady states exist only because Y folds complexes back into metabolites (N·v = Y·A·v = 0 with A·v ≠ 0).

4 · Complex activity over all steady states: M = A·K

Row c of M is the net formation rate of complex c along each steady-state direction. A zero row means the complex is never net formed or consumed in any steady state: it is balanced. Rows that are proportional mean the two complexes' activities keep a fixed ratio in every steady state: they are concordant, and each class is one colour. Both statements are read off the matrix directly.

Balanced: C, F, H, I. Metabolite F lives only in complex F; H and I likewise. C leaves as C+G and returns as C+E, both through the balanced complex H, so the two detours cancel.
Class 1 (B, D, D+E, B+G), rows ±(1, 1): the loop shared by both modes. B enters as complex B and returns inside B+G; D is made as D and leaves inside D+E. These hand-overs are why B and D are not balanced.
Class 2 (C+G, C+E), rows ±(1, 0): the return of G to E through H, active only in k1.
Class 3 (A+G, A+E), rows ±(0, 1): the same return through I, active only in k2.
In the unreduced network (14 reactions) K has five more columns, one per reversible pair; their columns of A·K are zero, so balance and concordance are unchanged. Complex A, reachable only through R1/R2, is balanced there for the same reason R1/R2 carries no net flux.
