Nullspace walkthrough

The example network of Langary et al. (2025), from the incidence matrix to absolute concentration robustness, one matrix per step. Every reaction direction is its own column (14 reactions, 13 complexes, 9 metabolites). That is the paper's convention and the one the kinetics needs: under mass action R1 runs at k1[A] and R2 at k2[B], and the fact that their net flux is always zero is not a dead end to discard but the statement k1[A] = k2[B], a fixed concentration ratio. Merging the pair into one net reaction keeps the flux picture and loses exactly that (section 7).

4 · Complex activity over all steady states: M = A·K

Row c of M is the net formation rate of complex c along each steady-state direction. A zero row is a balanced complex. Rows proportional to each other, with any sign, are concordant; a maximal group of them is a concordance module, shown by colour. Scaling each row by its first nonzero entry turns every module into one identical vector, so the modules are read off in one pass with no pairwise test. The reversible-pair columns are all zero: they never affect balance or concordance.

5 · From concordance to kinetic modules

Mass action in matrix form. Give each complex a concentration ψc = ∏s xsYsc, so ln ψ = Yᵀ ln x. Each reaction runs at its rate constant times its substrate complex's ψ, v = diag(k)·Sᵀψ with S the substrate part of A. Complex activity is then A·v = L·ψ with L = A·diag(k)·Sᵀ, a weighted Laplacian of the complex graph.

Upstream sets. Each concordance module is joined with all balanced complexes and pruned on the graph read from A: Phase I removes, to a fixed point, every complex produced from a substrate outside the set (autonomy); Phase II removes the terminal strong components (feeding).

Why an upstream set fixes concentration ratios. Autonomy means every reaction producing a complex of U starts in U, so the activities of U depend on ψU alone: AU·v = LU·ψU. From M, those activities point in one fixed direction λU scaled by a single number s: zero for the balanced members, the class-1 pattern for the others. Feeding means every complex of U drains out of it, which makes LU invertible (a Laplacian with leakage). So ψU = s·LU−1λU: all complex concentrations in U move together with s, in ratios fixed by the rate constants. For U = {A, B, C, D+E, F}:

Merging. Upstream sets that share a complex are one module; these three share none. Proposition S3-4 merges two sets whose representatives' composition difference lies in the span of the differences already fixed, equivalently whose signatures Lᵀy agree, with L = ker(YΔᵀ) over the three sets:

The sets through H and through I share a signature and merge, which also merges their two concordance modules. The network has deficiency δ = 2 and is not weakly reversible; one concordance merge leaves δ − 1 = 1, and Lemma S4-5 then places every unbalanced complex in one module. Its upstream set is the single kinetic module

{A, B, C, D+E, F, C+G, H, A+G, I}

with D, B+G, C+E and A+E, the terminal complexes, left as singletons.

6 · ACR and ACRR: the left nullspace of YΔ

Inside a kinetic module every ratio ψc/ψd is fixed, i.e. (yc − yd)·ln x is the same in every positive steady state. Collect these differences as the columns of YΔ. A metabolite has ACR when its unit vector lies in their span, which is a zero row of L = ker(YΔᵀ); two metabolites have ACRR when their rows of L are equal.

7 · What merging the directions would have cost

Merging each forward/reverse pair and cutting the directions no steady state uses leaves 8 reactions. R1/R2 disappears (zero net flux), and complex A with it. Balance (C, F, H, I) and concordance (the same three classes) come out unchanged: reversible pairs only ever add zero columns to M.
Kinetic modules do not. In the full network R1/R2 puts complex A into the first upstream set together with C, which fixes the difference A − C. That is exactly what makes the H-branch and the I-branch signatures equal (C+G − A+G = C − A), so they merge, the deficiency drops to one, and Lemma S4-5 builds the module of nine. Without R1/R2 complex A is gone, the three signatures differ, nothing merges, and three small modules remain, {B, C, D+E, F}, {C+G, H} and {A+G, I}, with no ACR at all. Net flux decides balance and concordance; the individual reaction directions decide the kinetics.
The benchmark networks keep every reversible reaction as two irreversible columns (1798 pairs on iCN900), so this loss never occurs there.
